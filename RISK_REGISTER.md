# Risk Register — Jeopardy Wagering Model

Last updated: 2026-07-23

Note (2026-07-23): risks #9 and #10 below document a two-layer defect in the
Daily Double engine's statistics — the first fix (risk #9) was itself found
wrong on a deeper review (risk #10) and re-fixed. Prior conclusions in this
register are not rewritten when superseded — each row keeps its own history so
the sequence of what changed and why stays legible. See the changelog and
`docs/reviews/statistical-review-2026-07-23-v2.md`.

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Status |
|---|------|-----------|--------|------------|--------|
| 1 | Historical data licensing restricts what appears in shipped artifacts | High | Medium | Cite Tesauro et al. (2013) published statistics for anything shipped; restrict jwolle1 dataset to internal validation only. See ADR-0001. | Accepted, mitigated |
| 2 | Real player confidence is unobservable in historical data, limiting how prescriptive the model can be | High | High | Require a self-reported confidence input (e.g., 1–5 scale mapped to historical row-based accuracy) rather than inferring confidence purely from data | Open — design decision needed |
| 3 | Live in-game wagering assistance would violate actual contest rules | N/A (policy) | High | Scope the tool explicitly as pre-game study and post-game analysis only; state this in the README and white paper | Open — needs explicit statement in shipped docs |
| 4 | Pairwise heuristic decomposition for 3-player wagers is an approximation, not a proven joint optimum | Medium | Medium | Validate heuristic layer against the full N-player Monte Carlo simulator before treating it as authoritative | Resolved — Tier 1 (`fj_wager.py`) uses a single largest-threat formula, not pairwise decomposition; see section 3.2 of the research memo |
| 5 | Tier 2 (`fj_bestresponse.py`) and the Daily Double engine (`dd_wager.py`, `simulate.py`) use illustrative, uncalibrated opponent-behavior and clue-success constants (see ADR-0002) | High | Medium | Every constant is named and documented as a placeholder; hand-derived tests use explicit fixed models, not the defaults, so they stay valid once real calibration replaces the defaults | Open — needs a calibration pass against Tesauro et al.'s full tables and/or the internal-only jwolle1 dataset |
| 6 | Monte Carlo win probabilities in the Daily Double engine carry sampling noise, which can make a worse wager look better by chance at low trial counts | Medium | Low | Implemented common random numbers (paired random draws across candidate wagers within each trial) to remove most of this noise; see comment in `dd_wager.py::analyze` | Mitigated — raise `trials_per_wager` for tighter estimates if still needed |
| 7 | Regular-clue and future-Daily-Double dynamics in `simulate.py` ignore buzzer competition and wrong-answer pass-through | Medium | Medium | Documented as a named simplification in the module docstring; results from multi-clue-ahead or multi-DD-ahead rollouts should be treated as rough, not precise | Open — refine before trusting rollouts far from the last Daily Double |
| 8 | `app/index.html` is a hand-written JavaScript port of the Python engine, not generated from it -- the two can silently drift apart as either one changes. The two engines also use different PRNGs (Python: Mersenne Twister; JS: mulberry32), so their Monte Carlo Daily Double outputs are only distributionally comparable, never identical trial-for-trial. | Medium | High | Re-checked 2026-07-23 (v2 review): extracted the `<script>` block and ran it under Node against the FJ hand-derived values, the strict-win tiebreak, the 3-way "both leaders bust" case, the inverse-normal and Bonferroni-band reference values, and the paired-SE <= independent-SE invariant -- all matched. Still a manual step, not automated in CI. | Open — add a CI step (once CI exists) that runs the shared cases through the JS engine and fails on mismatch; only the exact/deterministic functions can be checked for equality, MC outputs must be checked distributionally |
| 9 | Daily Double engine picked the "best" wager by comparing noisy Monte Carlo win-probability estimates with a fixed 1e-9 tolerance, treating them as exact. Reported 2026-07-23: a 2-point change in stated confidence (42% → 44%) swung the recommended wager from $350 to $4,200 for identical scores -- a real defect, not a data problem. | High (was live in shipped app) | High | First fix (2026-07-23, v1): standard-error-aware "plateau" selection. **Superseded by the v2 fix below — the v1 fix's SE formula was itself wrong (see risk #10). Kept here unrewritten for history.** | Resolved by v1, then found insufficient — see risk #10 |
| 10 | The v1 fix for risk #9 computed the wager-comparison standard error with the INDEPENDENT two-sample formula `sqrt(se_w^2 + se_best^2)`, but the simulator uses common random numbers (CRN), under which candidate wagers are strongly positively correlated. The independent formula therefore over-stated the comparison variance, producing a spuriously wide plateau and systematic UNDER-betting (it recommended ~$0 even where betting was clearly better; for `[4200,6000,3400]`@0.42 the independent rule recommended $100 where the CRN-correct rule recommended $4,200). | High (was live after the v1 fix) | High | Fixed 2026-07-23 (v2) in `engine/dd_wager.py` and ported to `app/index.html`: the comparison SE is now the PAIRED per-trial difference (candidate vs. empirical best), which respects CRN; and the band is a multiplicity-adjusted Bonferroni value `z* = Phi^{-1}(1 - alpha/k)` over the `k` comparisons-with-the-best (Hsu, 1996). Verified stable across seeds and neighbouring confidences on a flat scenario ($0) AND that it still recommends a positive wager on a clear-signal scenario ($1,200). See `docs/reviews/statistical-review-2026-07-23-v2.md`. | Resolved — Python and JS fixed; 33 tests passing incl. a paired-SE <= independent-SE invariant |
| 11 | The Daily Double engine's reported `win_probability` is the in-sample maximum over candidate wagers, so it is an upward-biased ("winner's curse") estimate of the true best win probability | Medium | Low | Documented in the engine docstring, the returned notes, and the v2 review. The *recommended wager* is well insulated (smallest wager on a multiplicity-controlled plateau, not the raw argmax); only the reported percentage is optimistic. | Accepted, documented — a debiased (data-split) estimate is the future fix |
| 12 | Tier 2 Final Jeopardy enumeration treats each opponent's correctness as independent, but real FJ correctness is positively correlated across players (shared clue difficulty) | Medium | Medium | Documented against ADR-0002 and in the v2 review. Positive correlation would raise joint "both miss" probabilities, so figures like the 1/12 "both leaders bust" example are conservative floors, not over-estimates. | Open — add an opponent-correctness correlation parameter and report sensitivity |
| 13 | The Tier 2 enumeration is described as "exact"; it is exact in the sense of no sampling error, but is computed in IEEE-754 double precision, not exact rational arithmetic | Low | Low | Accumulated floating error (~1e-16) is far below the 1e-9 comparison tolerance, so no result is affected; white-paper wording tightened to "exact enumeration in double precision (no sampling error)". | Accepted, documented |
